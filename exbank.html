The problem with paper specs

A generator that has never carried real load is a guess, not a guarantee.

Nameplate ratings tell you what a genset, transformer, or switchgear panel is supposed to do. Only a controlled load test tells you what it actually does — at full load, under realistic power factor, before commissioning sign‑off.

EX‑BANK exists for the moment between "built" and "trusted": the sea trial, the shipyard commissioning bay, the shore power acceptance test where failure isn't an option once the vessel or the plant is live.

How the load is built

Resistive heat. Inductive demand. Combined on purpose.

A resistive‑only bank proves a generator can make heat. It can't prove the generator holds voltage and frequency when a ship's motors, pumps, and HVAC pull current out of phase. EX‑BANK's R+L composite stages let commissioning engineers dial in a power factor that matches the real vessel or plant load — then watch the unit answer for it.
